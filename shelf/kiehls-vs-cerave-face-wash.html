Kiehl's Facial Fuel vs CeraVe Foaming Cleanser: is the $28 face wash worth it?

Short answerNot for what it does. Both wash off oil and dirt, and CeraVe costs about a third as much per ounce. What Kiehl's adds is a menthol tingle and caffeine on the label, and we found no study showing a rinse-off wash delivers anything from them. If you like the feel, that is a fair reason to pay; just know that is what you are paying for.

What the evidence says

The American Academy of Dermatology's advice on face washing is short: use a gentle, non-abrasive cleanser that does not contain alcohol, and limit washing to twice a day and after sweating. Neither product here is tested against the other, and neither brand discloses ingredient amounts.

On the labels: Kiehl's Facial Fuel cleans with sulfates (ammonium lauryl sulfate, sodium lauryl sulfate and related surfactants) and lists caffeine, menthol and fragrance. CeraVe Foaming Facial Cleanser uses a non-sulfate base and lists niacinamide, ceramides and hyaluronic acid, and is fragrance-free.

What this does not tell you

  • We compared labels and prices, not results on skin. No trial we found compares these two washes.
  • A face wash is on your skin for under a minute before you rinse it off, so ingredients that matter in a leave-on product may do little here. We did not find evidence either way for these specific washes.
  • Sulfate cleansers suit some people with very oily skin. If Kiehl's works for you and doesn't dry you out, it is not doing harm.
