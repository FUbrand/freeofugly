Creatine: which kind is worth buying

Short answerPlain creatine monohydrate is worth it. A third-party-tested version is worth the extra money if you are drug-tested. The buffered "Kre-Alkalyn" kind showed no edge in the one trial we found. We did not price it.

The full answer

Monohydrate is the form the trials were run on. The one head-to-head trial we found put a buffered creatine (Kre-Alkalyn) against monohydrate in 36 resistance-trained people for 28 days. Neither dose of Kre-Alkalyn, including the low dose the maker recommends, produced greater changes in muscle creatine, body composition, strength or anaerobic capacity than monohydrate. It also found no evidence of fewer side effects.

Where the premium wins: contamination is a documented risk. A 2004 study of 634 non-hormonal supplements bought in 13 countries in 2000 to 2001 found 94 of them (14.8%) contained anabolic-androgenic steroids that were not on the label. That study is old and was not about creatine in particular, so it does not tell you the rate for any product today. What it does show is why a banned-substance screen exists. NSF, an independent testing organization, says its Certified for Sport programme tests for 290 banned substances. We read Thorne's own page for the certification status of its creatine; we did not check NSF's certified-product listing. If your sport or job drug-tests you, the certification is what you are paying for, and it is worth roughly $0.15 to $0.22 a day, depending on the tub size you compare with.

If you are not tested, the contamination risk does not change what the creatine does for you. Plain monohydrate from a maker you trust is enough.

What this does not tell you

  • The Kre-Alkalyn trial was funded by AlzChem AG, which makes the monohydrate (Creapure) used as the comparison. The authors state they collected and analysed the data independently. It is one trial of 36 people for 28 days.
  • The steroid-contamination figure comes from supplements bought in 2000 to 2001. It is not a current rate and it does not say any creatine product is contaminated.
  • We are not rating Optimum Nutrition against Thorne. We priced one example of each kind. Other brands exist at other prices.
