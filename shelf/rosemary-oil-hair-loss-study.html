Rosemary oil for hair loss: what the study actually found

Short answerPromising, not proven. One 2015 trial found rosemary oil and 2% minoxidil gave similar hair counts after six months. It did not include a placebo group, so it cannot show that rosemary oil beats doing nothing.

The full answer

The claim usually cited for this is Panahi and colleagues, published in SKINmed in 2015: "Rosemary oil vs minoxidil 2% for the treatment of androgenetic alopecia: a randomized comparative trial." Patients with androgenetic alopecia (pattern hair loss) were randomly assigned to rosemary oil (50 people) or 2% minoxidil (50 people) for six months, with a standardised microphotographic hair count at baseline, 3 months and 6 months.

Results from the abstract: no significant change in mean hair count at 3 months in either group. At 6 months both groups had a significant increase compared with baseline. There was no significant difference between the groups at 3 or 6 months. Scalp itching rose from baseline in both groups and was more frequent in the minoxidil group.

What that supports: in this trial, rosemary oil did about as well as 2% minoxidil at six months. What it does not support: that rosemary oil works better than no treatment (there was no placebo or untreated arm), that it matches the 5% minoxidil sold to men, or that results last past six months. A trial with no placebo group cannot separate a treatment effect from other causes of change. "No significant difference" is also not the same as proven equal.

What this does not tell you

  • The abstract does not state the participants' sex or age, whether the trial was blinded, or what rosemary preparation was used, so we have not claimed any of those.
  • Men's Rogaine foam is sold at 5% minoxidil (Target listing). The trial's comparator was 2%.
  • American Academy of Dermatology (dermatologists' professional body) lists minoxidil and finasteride as the treatments for male pattern hair loss and notes that minoxidil's benefits are lost when you stop. Talk to a dermatologist before starting treatment.
