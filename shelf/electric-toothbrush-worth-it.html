The Verdict

Electric toothbrush vs manual: worth about $63 a year?

Short answerWorth it, narrowly. Powered brushes beat manual ones on plaque and gum inflammation in the best evidence we found. The size of the benefit for your actual teeth is unclear, and a manual brush used well also works.

The full answer

The Cochrane review of powered versus manual brushing pooled 56 trials with 5,068 randomised people. Compared with manual brushing, powered brushes reduced plaque by about 11% at 1 to 3 months and about 21% after 3 months. Gingivitis (gum inflammation) fell by about 6% and about 11%. The review rates the evidence as moderate certainty.

The same review says plainly that "the clinical importance of these findings remains unclear." The American Dental Association says both types of toothbrush can clean teeth effectively and thoroughly, and that choosing between them is a wash. So the honest framing is a measurable but modest edge, for roughly 14 to 17 cents a day more ($52 to $63 a year).

Free of Ugly's opinion, not from the review: it is likelier to be worth it if you brush quickly or inconsistently, and easier to skip if your technique is already good.

What this does not tell you

  • The review's brush-type subgroups did not explain the differences between trials. The largest group (27 trials) tested rotation-oscillation heads. We priced an Oral-B model as one example; a different powered brush may perform differently and cost differently.
  • The review we read was published in 2014, with searches to January 2014. We did not check whether a later update exists.
  • Our cost model uses assumptions we chose, listed in the math below. Change them and the answer moves a little.
