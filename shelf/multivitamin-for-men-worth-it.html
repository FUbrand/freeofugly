Multivitamin for men: what the evidence says and what it costs

Short answerDon't count on a multivitamin to protect your heart or prevent cancer: the US Preventive Services Task Force says the evidence is insufficient either way. If you want one anyway, a basic one at about 10 cents a day is a low-cost choice. The premium one also carries the NSF Certified for Sport seal, which signals independent banned-substance testing (per Thorne's page), which matters if you are drug-tested; we did not compare the formulas, so we cannot say the certification explains the price.

The full answer

In June 2022 the US Preventive Services Task Force concluded that the current evidence is insufficient to assess the balance of benefits and harms of multivitamins for preventing cardiovascular disease or cancer in healthy adults. That is an "I statement". It does not say multivitamins do not work. It says the trials do not settle it. The same statement recommends against beta carotene and vitamin E supplements for those purposes (grade D, the task force's "recommends against" rating).

It applies to community-dwelling, non-pregnant adults. It does not apply to people with a known nutritional deficiency, who should be treated for that deficiency.

On price, we priced two real examples. Centrum Men, 120 tablets, is $12.29 at Target, one tablet a day, about 10 cents. Thorne Basic Nutrients 2/Day is $40 for 30 servings of two capsules, $1.33 a day, and the Thorne page says it carries the NSF Certified for Sport seal (independent testing for banned substances). These are different formulas, so compare the labels for what you actually want. One thing to check: retailer listings for Centrum Men show beta-carotene among its ingredients, and the task force recommends against beta-carotene supplements for preventing heart disease or cancer (grade D, the task force's "recommends against" rating). We did not verify the bottle's label, so read it. We did not find evidence that a $40 multivitamin outperforms a $12 one, but we also did not find a trial comparing them.

What this does not tell you

  • The statement is about preventing heart disease and cancer. Multivitamins were not assessed here for other outcomes.
  • An I statement means the evidence is lacking, not that it is negative.
  • We did not compare ingredient lists in detail. Thorne's capsules and Centrum's tablet differ in what they contain.
