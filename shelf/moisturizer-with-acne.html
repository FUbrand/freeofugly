Moisturizer when you have acne: what dermatologists say and what is not proven

Short answerA daily moisturizer can help your skin tolerate drying acne treatments. It supports the treatment; it does not replace it. The American Academy of Dermatology says: "These treatments tend to dry and irritate the skin. Using a moisturizer every day can help your skin tolerate these medications." If you moisturize, pick one labeled oil-free, non-comedogenic or won't clog pores. We found no controlled trial showing moisturizer clears acne on its own.

The full answer

The American Academy of Dermatology's acne page on moisturizer (the dermatology association's patient page) lists acne treatments such as benzoyl peroxide, salicylic acid and retinoids, and says: "These treatments tend to dry and irritate the skin. Using a moisturizer every day can help your skin tolerate these medications." Several of them, including benzoyl peroxide and salicylic acid, are sold over the counter, so this applies to a man using only a drugstore wash.

On what to buy, the same page says to look for a label that reads "Oil-free," "Non-comedogenic," or "Won't clog pores." Of those words it says: "When you see one of these descriptions, it means that the moisturizer is unlikely to cause acne."

The page also explains why dryness matters, in its own words: "When your skin becomes dry, your body makes more oil. The extra oil can clog your pores, which may lead to more breakouts." This is the Academy's explanation of the mechanism. It is not trial evidence, and we did not find a trial that tests it.

Not everyone is told to moisturize. The Academy's general page on picking a moisturizer (last updated 1/25/22), under oily skin, quotes Jason A. Clark, MD, a board-certified dermatologist in Atlanta: "Dr. Clark recommends considering skipping the moisturizer or opt for a gel, which is lighter than other types of moisturizer." That is one dermatologist's advice for oily skin on its general page, not the acne page. It is not advice for someone on a drying acne treatment, where the acne page says to moisturize daily.

Tonight: after you wash, put on a thin layer of a gel or lotion with one of those three label words.

What this does not tell you

  • We found no controlled trial of moisturizer versus no moisturizer in people with acne. Moisturizer supports treatment; the evidence here does not show it replaces treatment.
  • The small studies we found are open-label, run on people who were also on treatment, or made by industry. Isoda 2015: 4 weeks, 29 Japanese women with mild acne, a facial cleanser plus moisturizers and no acne medicine, no control group, products developed by the study team. It reported "The acne significantly decreased", but with no comparison group we cannot tell what moisturizer alone did.
  • The Academy's acne page says: "In studies, most patients see less acne between 4 and 8 weeks after beginning to use an acne friendly moisturizer." It also says: "All of the patients in these studies were following an acne treatment plan that required them to use acne medication, cleanser, and moisturizer." So those results cannot be credited to moisturizer alone.
  • One poster on the Academy's own reference list is described as "100% sponsored by Stiefel Laboratories" (an acne-drug maker).
  • Conflict of interest: the Academy's acne moisturizer page states "The American Academy of Dermatology gratefully acknowledges the support from Ortho Dermatologics", a company that makes acne drugs.
  • The Academy's moisturizer-choice page was last updated 1/25/22. The acne moisturizer page shows no update date.
  • Information, not medical advice. If your acne is not improving or your skin is raw or painful on treatment, see a dermatologist. We use no affiliate links on this page.
