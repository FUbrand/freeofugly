Price Per Use

What sunscreen costs per day at the dose dermatologists recommend

Short answerPer day, the price spread is wide: about $1.11 to $4.51. Nothing here shows the pricier tube protects you more: all three are labelled SPF 30 or higher, which is the minimum dermatologists advise (the American Academy of Dermatology, their professional body, also wants broad spectrum and water resistant; we did not check each label for both). The honest rule is the sunscreen you will wear every day at the right dose beats the one you ration. If the pricier one is the one you will actually wear daily, that is a fair reason to buy it.

The full answer

The American Academy of Dermatology recommends a broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher, at least 1 teaspoon on your face, and reapplication every two hours outdoors. A teaspoon is about 4.9 mL, or 0.167 US fluid ounces. At that dose a small tube does not last long: a 1.7 oz tube covers about 10 days of face use, and a 3 oz tube about 18.

Price per day at that dose runs from about $1.11 (CeraVe AM Lotion SPF 30) to $4.51 (EltaMD UV Clear SPF 46, bought direct). EltaMD also sells a 3.7 oz tube at $78, which works out to about $3.51 a day. That is a $3.40 a day gap between the cheapest and the dearest, roughly $1,240 a year, if you use it every day at the dermatologist-recommended dose. Using Target's same-day sale price for EltaMD ($34.99, $3.43 a day) the gap is $2.32 a day, about $847 a year. The model assumes one application a day; dermatologists also advise reapplying every two hours outdoors, which would cost more.

Does the price buy anything? It can buy a texture you prefer, and a sunscreen you like is one you wear. All three products are labelled SPF 30 or higher. We did not find evidence in this check that any of them protects better at equal dose. If the price makes you use less than a teaspoon, you are getting less protection from the tube you paid more for.

Fairness note: EltaMD, La Roche-Posay and CeraVe are real products with real fans and we are not ranking their formulas, only their cost per day at the dermatologist-recommended dose.

What this does not tell you

  • Tube sizes are in ounces as labelled. We treat 1 oz as 1 fluid ounce, so a dense cream may hold slightly less volume and run out sooner.
  • Prices are from three different places on one day: CeraVe from cerave.com, EltaMD from eltamd.com, La Roche-Posay from a Target search page. Retail prices and sales differ. Target listed EltaMD UV Clear 1.7 oz at a sale price of $34.99 the same day.
  • SPF numbers differ across the three (30, 60, 46). We did not compare UVA protection or ingredients.
  • The dermatologist-recommended dose is a minimum for the face. Many people also cover the neck and ears.
