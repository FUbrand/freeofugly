The Verdict

Biotin for hair growth: skip it unless you are actually low

Short answerSkip it. Biotin may help hair when you are deficient or have a specific condition. For a healthy person with ordinary hair loss, the evidence is not there, and biotin can interfere with some blood tests.

The full answer

A 2017 systematic review searched PubMed (with listed search terms) for case reports and randomised trials of biotin for hair and nails. It found 18 reported cases. In all of them, the patient had an underlying pathology that explained poor hair or nail growth, and all showed improvement after biotin. The authors conclude that biotin may help in biotin deficiency and in conditions like brittle nail syndrome or uncombable hair, that those cases are uncommon, and that there is a lack of sufficient evidence for supplementation in healthy individuals.

The American Academy of Dermatology page on male pattern hair loss describes minoxidil and finasteride as the treatments it covers. We read it for that; we are not claiming it addresses biotin.

Then there is the lab-test issue. The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) states it has continued to receive adverse event reports indicating that biotin interference caused falsely low troponin results (page current as of June 2022). Troponin is the blood test used to help diagnose a heart attack. If you take biotin, tell any doctor who orders bloodwork.

What this does not tell you

  • The 2017 review is a literature search of case reports and trials. It says supplementation lacks sufficient evidence, which is different from proving it has zero effect.
  • We did not verify the exact dose at which interference starts. The FDA page we read gives no dose figure.
  • This is information, not medical advice. If you think you are deficient, a blood test and a doctor are the way to find out.
