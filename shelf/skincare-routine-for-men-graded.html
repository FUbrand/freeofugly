The Graded Routine

Skincare routine for men, graded by the evidence

Short answerDaily sunscreen is the step with the strongest evidence, and a gentle cleanser and a moisturizer are what dermatologists advise. A prescription retinoid has trial support. Niacinamide has one small trial, and beta-carotene pills did nothing for skin aging.

How the grades work

These grades are FOU's own judgment of the sources below, not an official scale. A means a large randomised trial (900+ adults here) that is also backed by AAD guidance; only sunscreen qualifies. G means AAD guidance with no trial cited. B means several consistent randomised trials. C means one trial. 0 means a trial that found no overall benefit. These are not the USPSTF's letters. This is an evidence ladder, not a morning-and-night order: it mixes prevention (sunscreen), treatment (tretinoin) and appearance (niacinamide).

Step by step

  • Daily broad-spectrum sunscreen (A). The AAD advises a broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher, at least 1 teaspoon for the face, reapplied every two hours outdoors. In the Nambour trial, 903 adults under 55 were randomised to daily broad-spectrum sunscreen or to using it when they chose to, and the daily group showed no detectable increase in skin aging over 4.5 years. Skin aging was 24% less than in the discretionary group (relative odds 0.76, 95% CI 0.59 to 0.98, so the benefit could be small; the abstract lists the trial's power to detect moderate effects as "modest"). The abstract does not state an SPF number.
  • Mild cleanser (G, guidance). The AAD advises washing your face daily and after exercise with a mild cleanser and lukewarm water, and fragrance-free or non-comedogenic products depending on your skin.
  • Moisturizer (G, guidance). The AAD advises moisturizing daily, right after bathing or shaving, while the skin is damp.
  • Prescription tretinoin (B). Ask a dermatologist about it. A 2022 systematic review of 7 randomised trials found topical tretinoin consistently improved wrinkling, mottled pigmentation, sallowness and age spots from as early as 1 month. Four trials included only women and the rest were mostly women, and formulas and outcomes varied.
  • Niacinamide 5% (C). A 2004 12-week, double-blind, split-face trial of 50 women aged 40 to 60 found improvements in fine lines, spots, redness and sallowness. The authors are Procter & Gamble employees. A 2005 paper from the same group reports a similar design and may overlap; we count it as one body of evidence.
  • Beta-carotene pills (0). In the same Nambour trial, beta-carotene supplements had no overall effect on skin aging, though the authors saw contrasting associations in subgroups and say further study is needed. Separately, the USPSTF recommends against beta-carotene supplements for preventing heart disease or cancer.

What this does not tell you

  • The Nambour trial was run in Queensland, Australia (latitude 26 degrees south), and had some missing outcome data (the authors note it). Results may differ in a cloudier climate.
  • We did not grade other products people ask about, such as toners, charcoal washes or red-light devices. Absence from this list is not a verdict.
  • The AAD men's skin care page did not mention retinoids, and the AAD reviewed date on that page is not shown. Retinoids are listed here from the trial review, not AAD guidance.
